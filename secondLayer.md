What the Engine Needs to Create
1. Seller/Broker Outreach Email

The email should be tailored by business type.

Example for 100% seller-financed plumbing business:

Hi [Broker/Seller Name],

I saw the listing for [Business Name] in [Location]. I’m interested because it appears to be an established essential-service business with durable demand, recurring/repeat customers, and possible seller financing.

I’m especially interested in opportunities where the seller cares about a smooth transition for employees, customers, and the legacy of the business.

Could you send the NDA/CIM if available, and clarify whether the seller would consider a full or majority seller-financed structure for a qualified buyer?

I’d also like to better understand:
- Owner’s weekly role
- Employee/license continuity
- Revenue by service type
- Customer concentration
- Three-year financial history
- Seller transition expectations

Best,
Ben
2. Buyer Credibility Blurb

The system should generate a short paragraph about you.

Ben Stone is an MBA with an accounting and finance background, including controller-level experience, operational controls, financial reporting, and automation. He is looking to acquire a durable, service-based local business with strong cash flow, fair seller financing, and a smooth transition plan that protects employees, customers, and the seller’s legacy.

This matters because retiring owners often care about who is buying, not just price.

3. Seller Financing Proposal

For any deal you like, the engine should create 2–3 financing structures.

Example:

Option A — Full Seller Carry
Purchase Price: $180,000
Down Payment: $0–$10,000
Seller Note: $170,000–$180,000
Rate: 6%–8%
Term: 5–7 years
Payments: monthly
Transition: 90–180 days

Option B — Small Down Payment + Seller Note
Down Payment: 10%
Seller Note: 90%
Rate: 6%–8%
Term: 5 years

Option C — Performance-Protected Offer
Down Payment: 5%–10%
Seller Note: 70%–80%
Earnout/Holdback: 10%–20% tied to retained revenue
